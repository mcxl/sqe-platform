Approved domain rules:
1. The evidence provider and accountable role are separate. One evidence item can support several audit questions through independently approved relationships.
2. Accountability stays with a stable job role. A dated role assignment records the named role-holder.
3. One risk can have several controls. One control can manage several risks. The auditor decides on each relationship separately.
4. Evidence can support, weaken or contradict an audit question. The auditor records a rationale for each classification.
5. Missing, unavailable, stale or inadequate evidence is an evidence gap. A gap is not automatically a finding.
6. Implementation and effectiveness conclusions are separate. A control can be implemented without being effective.
7. MATE assesses control design only. Separate evidence reviews support implementation and effectiveness conclusions.
8. ACE never overwrites an approved relationship. A change creates a new version and preserves previous decisions.
9. A revised conclusion becomes a new proposal version. Earlier conclusions and auditor decisions remain visible.
10. An unresolved contradiction or open material gap blocks a substantive conclusion. The auditor can approve Not Determined with an explicit limitation.

Approved trigger: The auditor must select an active Engagement before capture. Detailed relationships are added during Evidence Review.
Approved Engagement rule: Several Engagements can remain open, but each auditor session has one selected capture context.
Approved scope rule: Scope can be edited while Draft. After activation, a change creates a new approved version before more capture.
Approved Evidence Review rules: Reviewed evidence can remain in a visible Unlinked queue. Completed corrections create new review versions.
Approved Relationship Review rules: Use a visible queue ordered by material risk, then oldest waiting proposal. The auditor can select another proposal. Show one Proposed Relationship at a time. Show both linked records, the relationship type, source support, auditor rationale and version history together. Warn when two proposals link the same records with the same relationship type. Do not merge or delete possible duplicates automatically. Show open Evidence Gaps and unresolved contradictions before the decision. The auditor can save a draft, but a draft makes no Auditor Decision and creates no Approved Relationship. The auditor selects Approved, Rejected or Changes Required and records a written reason. Warnings do not decide automatically. Changes Required creates a new Relationship Version and preserves the previous version and decision. Only approval of the current Relationship Version creates an Approved Relationship. A decision for an earlier version cannot approve a later version. Each decision creates an immutable audit event with the auditor, date, time, Relationship Version, decision and reason. A failed save leaves the proposal undecided. A retry creates no duplicate decision or event.
Approved MATE rules: Assess one Control at a time. Start only after its links to the Binding Obligation, Risk and Accountable Role are approved. Show all four records together before the assessment. MATE does not approve these relationships. Show one check at a time in the fixed order Mandate, Accountability, Trigger and Escalation. Show progress, such as Check 2 of 4. The auditor can return to an incomplete check. An approved decision stays fixed. Each check shows its controlled question, proposed Yes or No answer, rationale, supporting evidence, weakening evidence, contradictory evidence, gaps, limitations, unresolved conflicts, source status and passage references. ACE does not select the answer. For each check, the auditor selects Approved, Rejected or Changes Required and records a written note. Approval requires sufficient evidence. The approved answer must match the current proposal. A different answer requires Changes Required and a new proposal version. ACE blocks completion when a check has no current source, evidence is insufficient, a conflict remains unresolved, a check is missing or records do not match. The auditor can save incomplete work as a draft. Missing evidence does not automatically mean No. After all four checks are approved, ACE uses the existing evaluator. All Yes answers produce Adequate. Mandate or Accountability as No produces Inadequate. Two or more No answers produce Inadequate. Only Trigger or Escalation as No produces Partially Adequate. The auditor cannot edit the rating. ACE creates one frozen Approved MATE Assessment. It records the Control, decisions, answers, rating, evidence references, auditor and review times. ACE links it to the approved Control trace. It does not create an audit conclusion. ACE never edits an approved assessment. Changes to the Control, evidence or answers start a new draft assessment version. ACE preserves all earlier assessments, ratings and decisions. Each MATE decision creates an immutable audit event. A failed save creates no partial decision. A retry creates no duplicate decision or event. A visible MATE queue shows ready, draft, blocked and approved assessments. ACE orders active work by material risk, then waiting time. The auditor can select another Control. Only the authenticated auditor makes MATE decisions. Real client information remains blocked at G0. AI can organise evidence and propose answers. AI cannot approve answers, change rating rules or approve conclusions.
Approved Conclusion Review rules: Start from one Accepted Planning Trace with its Approved MATE Assessment and rating. Require one approved Main, Implementation and Effectiveness Question for the same Control. Show the full trace, MATE rating and three questions before review. MATE provides context but does not decide implementation or effectiveness. Keep Implementation Review separate from Effectiveness Review. Each review has its own question, evidence matrix, classifications, gaps, contradictions, proposal and Auditor Decision. Evidence used in both reviews needs separate matrix entries and rationales. Each matrix shows the Evidence ID, precise source, origin, collection date, validity period, freshness, relevance reason, assumptions and limitations. Classify origin as Raw, Derived or Auditor Authored. Classify freshness as Current, Stale, Superseded or Uncertain. Classify relevance as Supports, Weakens or Contradicts. Derived evidence links to its raw source. Origin does not decide weight or sufficiency. Show every Evidence Gap and contradiction. Record each gap's explanation, materiality and disposition. Resolved and Accepted Limitation gaps require an auditor rationale. An open material gap or unresolved contradiction blocks a substantive conclusion. Neither automatically creates a negative conclusion. Implementation outcomes are Implemented, Partially Implemented, Not Implemented and Not Determined. Effectiveness outcomes are Effective, Partially Effective, Ineffective and Not Determined. Not Determined states the evidence limitation. It is not a pass or failure. Show one exact Conclusion Proposal version with its outcome, evidence, gaps, reasoning, assumptions and limitations. The auditor selects Approved, Rejected or Changes Required. Record a review note and final evidence-sufficiency decision. Approval must match the current proposal. A different outcome requires a new proposal version. Implementation and Effectiveness need separate decisions. Effectiveness depends on Implementation. Implemented or Partially Implemented can proceed to a substantive Effectiveness conclusion. Not Implemented or Not Determined requires Effectiveness to be Not Determined. ACE blocks inconsistent pairs. The auditor still approves Effectiveness separately. A substantive conclusion requires sufficient evidence, current relied-upon evidence, no unresolved contradiction and no open material gap. Not Determined requires insufficient or unresolved evidence and an explicit limitation. ACE creates no final record until both conclusions are approved. It returns no partly accepted record. After all gates pass, ACE creates one frozen Accepted Evidence-to-Conclusion Record. It contains the trace, MATE rating, questions, matrices, evidence, gaps, contradictions, limitations, proposal versions, decisions and outcomes. A later change creates new versions. ACE preserves the earlier record. A visible queue shows ready, draft, blocked, awaiting-one-decision and accepted records. ACE orders active work by material risk, then waiting time. Each final decision creates one immutable audit event. A failed save creates no partial decision. A retry creates no duplicate decision or event.
Approved reasoning method: Use the Requirement-Evidence-Reasoning Chain. Record the Requirement, Question, Claim, Evidence, Reasoning Link, Alternative, Challenge, Limits and Auditor Decision. For material fictional cases, pilot separate AI guidance roles for requirement interpretation, evidence analysis, alternative outcomes, operational context and traceability. AI role agreement gives no approval or independent assurance. When legal applicability is disputed, specialist competence is required or consequences are high, pilot a Human Review Council chaired by the Accountable Auditor. Record views before discussion and keep disagreement visible. Ordinary challenge stays inside Conclusion Review. Formal CONTRA remains separate until SQE approves it.
Approved Conclusion authority: AI can reference approved requirements and provide traceable guidance, reasoning and alternatives. Human specialists can advise. The Accountable Auditor decides applicability, evidence sufficiency and every conclusion. AI agreement and council votes cannot approve records. Real client information remains blocked at G0.
